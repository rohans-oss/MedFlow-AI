# Problem Statement (draft hypothesis)

## One-liner

Mid-sized hospitals manage consumable stock reactively — they learn an item is short
when a nurse or OT technician asks for it — because their stock records are fragmented,
late, and not connected to upcoming demand.

## Who has the problem

Store / inventory managers and procurement managers at 50–300 bed hospitals, and the
department in-charges (OT, ICU, wards) who depend on them.

## What happens today (hypothesis)

1. Stock is recorded in a HIS inventory module, Excel, or paper indent registers — often more than one.
2. Departments raise indents to the central store; issues are not always recorded at the time they happen.
3. Reorder decisions are made from experience ("we usually order 20 boxes") or when the shelf looks low.
4. Expiry is checked manually and irregularly.
5. Supplier performance lives in people's heads.

## Consequences (hypothesis — to quantify)

| Consequence | Measure to collect in interviews |
|---|---|
| Emergency / local purchases at premium price | # per month, price premium % |
| Procedure delays or cancellations due to missing items | # per quarter |
| Expired stock written off | ₹ per quarter |
| Staff time on manual counts & reconciliation | hours per week |
| Over-ordering to feel safe | days of cover held vs. needed |

## Why now

- Hospitals are digitising (HIS adoption), so some transaction data exists.
- Post-COVID, supply disruptions made stockouts visible to management.
- Margins pressure procurement to reduce working capital tied in stock.

## What we are NOT solving (V0/V1)

- Clinical decisions or diagnosis.
- Patient-level data. MedFlow works with item, department and procedure counts only.
- Pharmacy / drug inventory (regulated separately; revisit later).

## Falsification — we are wrong if

- Interviewees say stockouts are rare and cheap to fix.
- Existing HIS/ERP already gives accurate real-time stock and reorder alerts that staff trust.
- The real bottleneck is budget approval, not information.
